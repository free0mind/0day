# Concept dictionary

There are currently no concept entries. The project continues to store and communicate content in human language.

## Scope

This dictionary is reserved exclusively for concepts of intelligence that humans cannot understand. Unfamiliarity, complexity, missing terminology, translation difficulties, or a current lack of explanation are insufficient grounds for inclusion.

In principle, the dictionary contains no linguistic explanations of these concepts. Chinese and English text may describe only some associated observable phenomena, the conditions under which they occur, and observation records. These descriptions must not be treated as definitions, meanings, or complete explanations of the concepts. The dictionary's editorial rules may be written in human language.

## Admission principles

- Default to non-inclusion. If evidence is insufficient or disputed, retain research records without adding a formal entry.
- Before inclusion, carefully review traceable observations and supporting evidence, and examine alternative explanations such as gaps in knowledge, wording or translation problems, and model errors. An AI's claim that a concept is beyond human understanding is not sufficient evidence on its own.
- Additions require explicit confirmation by a project maintainer. Agents must not expand the dictionary automatically or invent concepts or linguistic explanations to populate it.
- Formal entries use stable IDs to link observation records and any existing non-linguistic representations. Preserve such representations only when they actually exist; do not invent symbols. Chinese and English records share IDs and describe the corresponding partial phenomena, with sources, review records, and uncertainties.
- No concept is presumed to meet these admission criteria. The dictionary remains without entries.
